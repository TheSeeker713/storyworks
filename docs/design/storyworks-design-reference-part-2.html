2. Blog module & the "Set aside" mechanic

Blog's defining feature is the review-gated stage workflow (Research → Outline → Draft → Edit), with AI-generated content held in a distinct sandboxed state until explicitly approved. Non-blocking was confirmed as a standing rule: ignoring an AI suggestion never traps the writer, and it never gets silently discarded either.

Research Outline Draft Edit 1 set aside

Local-first isn't a privacy feature bolted onto a cloud app. It changes what the software is allowed to promise you in the first place.

You keep writing right here, nothing waits on you to decide.

Setting a suggestion aside moves it out of the writing surface into a small badge in the stage strip, retrievable anytime by clicking it. Never forced, never lost.

6. PENS, vision and the roadmap decision

Scope correction, confirmed

PENS needs a genuinely robust intelligence system (tracked state, relationship variables, real condition evaluation) plus a dedicated React Three Fiber / WebGL visual layer specific to this module, a deliberate, isolated exception to the "WebGL stays on the bezel only" rule locked earlier. Combined, this is complex enough to roughly double the build time of everything else in the app. Decision: PENS ships as a visible roadmap item only. The tray icon stays present. Clicking it shows an honest, warm "on the roadmap" explanation, not an error state, not hidden.

⋮
PENS is on the roadmap
A real procedural, nonlinear story editor is a genuinely large build, its own intelligence system, its own visual layer. We wanted it visible now rather than hidden, so you know it's coming, not forgotten.
The full node-graph vision (Planning/Writing/Critique modes, condition-gated branches reading tracked project state) stays as real future-reference material for whenever PENS gets its dedicated phase, not near-term build scope.

10. Research findings this batch

10a. Day One competitive research

Full feature set confirmed: rich media, map view, On This Day, streaks, templates, auto-logged metadata, tags/search, IFTTT/Siri automation, print-to-book, cross-platform sync, an AI tier (Day One Gold).

The real, documented gap

Multiple independent 2026 privacy audits rank Day One Tier C on privacy: entries live on Automattic's servers, third-party analytics SDKs transmit usage data on launch, AI features route journal text to external servers including OpenAI, and encryption wasn't even present at launch. A documented backlash pattern exists specifically against AI in journaling (Google's Journal app is the clearest example), sharper than other writing categories because journaling processes grief, breakups, and anxiety. One competitor's founder's own words: "with the rise of cloud AI, I started getting seriously paranoid about where my deeply personal data was actually going." Combined with subscription fatigue ($49.99/yr, no monthly option), this confirms local-first, AI-optional, one-time-purchase isn't just consistent with Journal, it's the single most valuable thing Journal can offer that Day One structurally cannot.

10b. Local encryption recovery pattern

Cryptomator's model confirmed as the right precedent: a one-time, human-readable recovery key, independent of the password, no server or account needed. Notesnook's email-based recovery was reviewed and explicitly rejected, it requires a server/account, contradicting local-first, no-account philosophy.

10c. Offline map stack

MapLibre GL (MIT) + OpenStreetMap (ODbL) + PMTiles (offline-native format) confirmed as a real, working, fully open-source stack. OpenFreeMap offers free pre-generated global tiles. Organic Maps and CoMaps are real shipped precedents for exactly this privacy posture on macOS.

10d. Sidecar feasibility

Confirmed as a system-level macOS/iPadOS feature, not something requiring custom engineering. "All Mac apps work with Sidecar." Reframes iPad+Pencil drawing from "build a mirroring feature" to "make sure the canvas handles Pencil input well." A dedicated privacy audit of Sidecar itself wasn't found in this research pass, resolved as unnecessary for now since the disclosure is liability framing, not a technical safety claim.

11. Future phases, updated log

Carried forward from Part One (AI Audio/Podcast, Skins, Paper-physics cards, In-app clipboard history, Import/Export folded into Phase 8), plus one addition:

Standalone iPad companion app

Explicitly distinct from Sidecar mirroring. A genuine native iPadOS app would mean its own build target, a touch-first interface (the hover-dependent tray and right-click menus wouldn't translate as-is), its own vault sync mechanism, and a platform-scope expansion beyond the "macOS-only" rule locked in Phase 0. Not touched yet, logged for its own future research pass.
