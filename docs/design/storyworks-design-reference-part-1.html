1. Product thesis & target customer

Storyworks is a local-first, macOS-only creative writing suite built around six purpose-built tools — Novel, Screenplay, Blog, Notes, Journal, and PENS (Procedural Editor for Nonlinear Storytelling) — each with real, active AI agentic assistance woven directly into the writing experience.

The AI isn't a bolted-on assistant panel, it's a working creative partner the user can lean on as heavily as they want, sandboxed until a human explicitly approves anything it produces into the real manuscript. It's fully optional: every tool works completely by hand, with a permanent, one-click master switch to turn AI off entirely. The writer owns their work outright — plain .md files in a folder they choose, versioned automatically, never held hostage by a subscription or scraped for someone else's training data.

Target customer

Individual creative writers, not high-volume commercial content marketers and not enterprise workspace users. Three real sub-segments:

SegmentShareCares most about
Money-motivated indie authors~40%Speed, Codex-driven series consistency, export/publish compliance
Hobbyists & personal-expression writers~35% combinedPrivacy, ownership, zero or low cost, no pressure to use AI
Recognition-seekers~16%Polish and finishing, moderate AI adoption

All three are underserved by the market's current split between AI-first/format-weak tools and format-strong/AI-absent tools — that gap runs through every one of Storyworks' six tools, not just one.

3. Pain-point-driven design principles

Organized as want vs. need, per Storyflow's four-job framework (Develop · Draft · Organize · Publish), grounded in real market research, not assumption.

LiquidGlass / WebGL, resolved

  • A real WebGL glass-refraction library (@ybouane/liquidglass) was tested live via CDN for button hover/press effects.
  • Three attempts in the visualizer sandbox failed to render visibly despite the library loading and initializing correctly — diagnosed as a sandbox/iframe limitation, not a flaw in the library itself (it already runs correctly on myceliainteractive.com in a real browser).
  • Decision: CSS depth stays the standard for all buttons, everywhere. LiquidGlass is reserved specifically for the gold bezel, to be verified for real inside an actual dev build later.

Left edge (confirmed, offered as a real onboarding preference, changeable in Settings). A thin always-visible edge tab reveals the full tray on hover or click, following GitLab's documented pattern: a ~150ms delay before reveal, a buffer before closing, and a pin option to keep it open. 10. Codex, full system

10a. Reading an entry, progressions

Entries change over time via progressions, bound to a specific manuscript point, in two modes: addition (layers new detail on) or replacement (marks old detail void). Future progressions stay visible to the human author but are walled off from the AI's context until the story reaches that point.

11. Build your story & scratchpad

Build your story, question flow

A separate, optional, per-story onboarding wizard. Short questions (Title, Genre, Main character, Setting), fully rearrangeable via a clickable stepper. Leaving a field blank and pressing Enter triggers a graceful skip with a varied, rotating reassurance message, a deterministic "dummy AI" mechanic, no real inference needed. Exits via icon, Esc, or click-outside.

Scratchpad, the real first step

Precedes Title/Genre/Character entirely. A full-card freewrite surface with a dummy mic icon (placeholder for future STT). Detected candidates pool into a left-side panel that doesn't exist until the first item is found. Broad keyword/noun detection stays a cheap, instant, always-on heuristic; reformulating chaotic wording into structured sentences is honestly a job for a light AI pass, not pure pattern matching. Full corrected layout and live mechanic shown in Part Two.

Crash recovery

Confirmed principle: SQLite acts as a fast write-layer cache in front of slower .md saves, so a crash between two saves loses nothing. Banner design shown in Part Two.

12. Logged future phases

AI Audio, Podcast & Audiobook

Local, offline-first agentic pipeline. Chapter-by-chapter TTS with a master stitching service. Separate cast models per character plus a narrator model. Podcast format: two hosts, room for guests. Needs its own audio-model research pass.

Skins system

Daily-rotating background images with a fixed-opacity light overlay. Toggleable, decided during onboarding, adjustable in Settings with categories, import, and live brightness/contrast/opacity preview.

Paper-physics card interactions

WebGL/Three.js pickup-and-drop card physics with tilt, and a crumple-and-toss animation on delete. Genuine technical spike required before design work.

In-app clipboard history

System-wide Ctrl+C capture while the app is open, browsable history, local-only, easy purge. Privacy mechanic: the system flags content that looks sensitive and asks the user in the moment, "this looks personal, discard?", never a silent block-list.

Import/Export (Phase 8)

Import of .txt, .md, .doc, .docx, .pdf. Natural pairing with the already-planned Export/Publish phase (EPUB/PDF/Fountain/.fdx).

Standalone iPad companion app

Distinct from Sidecar mirroring. A genuine native iPadOS app, its own build, its own touch-first interface, its own vault sync, a real platform-scope expansion. Not touched yet. Full detail in Part Two.
